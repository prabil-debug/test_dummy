## Compliance guardrails (found by search; confirm on the primary sources before the first post)
- SEBI: investor EDUCATION is allowed; specific buy/sell calls, stock tips, performance claims or trading signals are advice and need SEBI registration, even when labelled "education". SEBI also barred regulated entities (brokers, AMCs...) from associating with unregistered advisers except pure education. Live stock prices are not allowed in educational posts. Sources: taxmann.com, business-standard.com (2025-01-30 circular coverage), foxmandal.in, mondaq.com. Primary: sebi.gov.in circulars (to check).
- ASCI (Addendum, 7 Apr 2025 per secondary sources): influencers giving advice on finance need credentials; investment advice needs SEBI registration shown next to the name; insurance-related advice needs relevant licence/qualification; GENERAL awareness content (e.g. why health insurance matters) is not the same as advice. Sponsored posts must carry clear disclosure. Primary: ascionline.in (to check).
- IRDAI advertising rules not yet checked.
- So the page's rule: teach concepts and general frameworks; NEVER name a specific stock, mutual fund, crypto coin, or insurer as "buy this"; no return promises; any growth example uses a clearly labelled ASSUMED rate; every Reel carries "പൊതുവായ അറിവ് മാത്രം, ഉപദേശമല്ല" on screen; sponsored posts disclosed. Recommend one consult with a CA / lawyer before the first paid collaboration.

## Honest expectations about sponsors
- A clear niche helps, but sponsors look at reach, completion, saves, comments and audience quality, not only the niche. A new page typically needs consistent posting for months before inbound offers; no timeline is promised.
- Finance sponsors are regulated and careful: education-only pages are the ones they CAN work with. Keeping advice out is also what keeps sponsor doors open.

## Content ladder (teach in this order; general education, not personal advice)
1. First salary: what to do before spending. 2. Health insurance first (why, what to look for). 3. Starter emergency fund (what counts, how many months is the common rule of thumb: VERIFY source). 4. Term life insurance (who needs it, vs endowment: general concept). 5. Debt: EMI and credit card traps. 6. Why invest at all: inflation. 7. Compounding with an ASSUMED rate. 8. SIP as a concept (no fund names). 9. Common scams and "guaranteed return" red flags. 10. Taxes basics (VERIFY each rule and year).
Common-order note: many educators suggest health cover and a small emergency buffer before investing; present it as "a common framework", not "what you must do".
