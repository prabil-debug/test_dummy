# What If the Internet Disappeared Forever?
Working title. Landscape 1920x1080, 2D stick-figure scenes, ~6 min.
Status: DRAFT 2 (user's script, elaborated). Items marked VERIFY must be checked against sources before recording.

## HOOK
Imagine waking up tomorrow morning.
You reach for your phone. No notifications. No messages. You open Instagram. Nothing.
You try Google. It doesn't load.
You restart your Wi-Fi. Still nothing.
At first, you think it's just a network problem.
But then you look outside.
Traffic lights are malfunctioning. Shops aren't accepting digital payments. Flights are being delayed. Banks are struggling to operate.
And then you hear something terrifying on the radio.
The internet is gone. Everywhere. And it's never coming back.
No explanation. No warning. No way to fix it.
What happens to the world when humanity loses the technology it has depended on for decades?
Let's find out.

## THE FIRST 24 HOURS
The first few hours would be confusing.
Billions of people would try to reconnect. Internet providers would be flooded with calls. Governments would issue emergency announcements through television and radio.
But the biggest shock wouldn't be social media disappearing.
It would be money.
Most people don't carry much physical cash anymore. Businesses depend on digital payment systems, online banking, and connected payment networks.
You walk into a supermarket, fill your shopping cart, and reach the checkout.
Your card doesn't work.
Neither does your payment app.
And the cashier tells you they can only accept cash.
For millions of people, this would be the first time they realise how dependent their daily lives have become on something they barely think about.

## IT HAS ALREADY HAPPENED, IN MINIATURE
Here's the part that surprises most people. This isn't pure fantasy.
In October 2021, Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp went down for about six hours. Billions of people lost them at once. Reports at the time said some engineers struggled to fix the problem because the systems they needed to get inside the buildings were affected too. VERIFY
In January 2011, Egypt's government cut off the country's internet for about five days. VERIFY
And in 2022, an undersea cable was cut after a volcanic eruption, and the island nation of Tonga was largely cut off for weeks. VERIFY
Those were small outages, in small places, with the rest of the world still online to help.
Now take away the rest of the world.

## THE WORLD STARTS SLOWING DOWN
By the second day, things could get much worse.
Modern businesses depend on connected systems to communicate, manage inventory, process orders, and coordinate deliveries.
Airlines rely on digital networks. Hospitals use connected systems to access records and coordinate care. Supply chains depend on constant information exchange.
Without the internet, these systems wouldn't all instantly collapse. Many have offline backups and independent communication networks.
But operations would become slower, more difficult, and less reliable.
Imagine a hospital trying to find a patient's medical history stored in a cloud system.
Imagine a supermarket that doesn't know when its next food delivery will arrive.
Imagine a factory that can't talk to its suppliers.
The world would still be moving.
But it would be moving almost blindly.

## A WORLD WITHOUT SOCIAL MEDIA
Now imagine something else.
No Instagram. No YouTube. No TikTok. No WhatsApp. No Netflix. No online gaming.
For the first time in decades, billions of people would lose their main connection to the digital world.
Content creators would lose their audiences overnight. Online businesses would struggle. Remote workers would have no way to reach their teams.
But something unexpected might happen.
People would start going outside more.
They would visit friends instead of texting them. Meet people instead of swiping through profiles. Read physical newspapers instead of scrolling through headlines.
Libraries, radio stations, television networks, and physical communities could become important again.
The world might become quieter.
But it wouldn't necessarily become happier.
Because for many people, the internet isn't just entertainment. It's how they work, learn, communicate, and stay connected to people they love.

## AFTER ONE MONTH
After a month, governments and businesses would be forced to adapt.
Radio would become incredibly important. Television broadcasts, telephone networks, postal services, and printed newspapers would help fill the gap.
People would go back to physical maps, cash, paper records, and face-to-face meetings.
Businesses would rebuild their systems around local networks and offline processes.
But rebuilding the internet wouldn't be as simple as plugging a few computers back in.
The internet is a global network of cables, data centres, servers, and software. A very large share of international data travels through undersea cables. VERIFY
Even if the internet itself disappeared, much of that physical equipment might still exist.
The real challenge would be restoring the software, the agreements, and the coordination that make it all work.
And without the global network, the world would become far less connected.

## AFTER ONE YEAR
One year later, humanity would probably look very different.
Some countries would adapt faster than others.
Local economies would become more important. International trade could become more complicated. Education would rely more heavily on classrooms, textbooks, and physical resources.
Science and research would slow down, because sharing information across countries would become much harder.
And here's something interesting.
A generation that grew up with instant access to almost all human knowledge would suddenly have to learn how to live without it.
No instant answers. No online tutorials. No searching for directions. No downloading information in seconds.
Knowledge would still exist. But finding it and sharing it would take far more effort.
Humanity wouldn't return to the Stone Age.
But the world would lose one of its most powerful tools for cooperation.

## THE ENDING
Here's the most interesting part.
The internet is not humanity.
We existed long before it.
We built cities, discovered medicines, explored space, and connected continents without it.
We would find ways to survive.
But we would lose something extraordinary.
The ability to communicate with almost anyone, anywhere, in seconds.
The ability to find decades of human knowledge with a simple search.
The ability to turn an idea into something the entire world can see.
So perhaps the real question isn't what would happen if the internet disappeared forever.
It's this.
Have we built a world that can no longer function without it?
And if the internet disappeared tomorrow, would you know how to live without it?
