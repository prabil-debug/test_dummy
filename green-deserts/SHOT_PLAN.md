# Shot plan: What If All Deserts on Earth Turned Completely Green?

Format: 1920x1080 landscape, 30 fps, 5:51 (about 10,530 frames).
Timings come from `voice/timeline.json` (sentence times) and `voice/words.json` (word times for captions). Narration is locked (Fenrir, v3 natural pacing).
Style: the same 2D stick-figure look as the earlier WHAT IF videos (flat colour, thick dark outlines, pastel backgrounds, one accent colour per section, word-by-word captions, small shakes on key words, slow push-in on every hold, cuts 2 frames before their word). Reuse the shared drawing kit and caption system from the earlier compositions.

## Guardrails for this topic (important)
- **No flags, no leaders, no political imagery.** The China and Africa sections show only landscapes, trees, tools and generic stick figures. No national symbols.
- **No precise national borders.** Draw continents and regions as soft, stylised shapes; label regions ("Sahara", "Sahel", "Amazon") with text only, no border lines.
- **Desert peoples are shown respectfully:** generic stick figures with a tent and a few animals; no costume stereotypes, no religious symbols, no "empty wasteland" framing. Show them as people with homes, not as props.
- **Animals are never in distress.** The "losers" scene shows animals calmly walking out of frame or looking at a changing horizon; no death, no suffering.
- **No real brand logos.** Solar and wind scenes use generic panels and turbines.
- **Balanced tone:** every cost is paired with a gain (more water, more farms, more carbon storage), so the video never says "greening deserts is simply good or bad".
- **Hypothetical, honest:** use "scientists think", "models suggest" wording on screen where the script does. The 2018 solar-and-wind result is labelled "computer model", not "measured".

## Recurring visual devices
- **The green wave:** a soft green wash that sweeps across sand dunes and leaves trees behind. It appears in the hook, on "turn green", and in the closing recap.
- **Sand-to-forest slider:** a horizontal slider with sand on the left and forest on the right, used for the rules, albedo and air sections.
- **Counters:** rolling numbers on their words (6,000 years, 5,000 years, 40%, one sixth, hundreds of litres, half the rain, hundreds to 1,000 years per inch, 28 million tonnes, 22,000 tonnes, 30-40% vs 8-15%, 9 million km², "more than double", 200-500 mm, 2.3 billion, 3 in 10, 100 million hectares, 4% vs 18%, 8 million hectares a year, 1978, 19 metres).
- **The orbit wobble:** a small Earth tilting on its axis with a monsoon arrow shifting north; reused in the twist.

## Dead air rules (from the silence audit)
Whenever the narration pauses 0.7 s or longer, something on screen keeps moving (the green wave drifting, a counter ticking, vapour arrows flowing, a slow push-in), and the sound bed or a soft effect carries the pause. The beats (after "Now imagine they came back.", "...on the other side of the world." and "The rains left.") may hold a quiet frame for about 1.2 s with a slow push-in.

## Build order (Claude Code on the Mac)
1. Build sections 1-3 (0:00-1:10) as the **look test**: render 6 half-size stills and wait for approval.
2. Build the remaining sections back to back, then show 10-12 stills across them.
3. Sound mix, then a **half-resolution draft render** to check motion, then ask before the full render.
4. Full render (lower the final audio peak so the AAC encode stays below -1 dBFS), frame-by-frame quality check, then the SRT subtitle file.
