# The Bridge That Wobbled (Millennium Bridge, London)

Status: script APPROVED by user. Facts below must be verified against sources before recording.

## Facts (verify)
- Opened 10 June 2000, closed 12 June 2000.
- Steel suspension footbridge over the Thames. Engineer: Arup. Architect: Foster and Partners. Sculptor: Anthony Caro.
- Huge opening-day crowds; reports of up to ~2,000 people on the bridge at once.
- Sway was lateral (sideways).
- Cause: pedestrians unconsciously widened their stance to balance, synchronising footsteps with the sway.
- Reopened February 2002 after dampers were fitted (about 90; viscous + tuned-mass).
- Nickname: "Wobbly Bridge".

## Script (~190 words, ~55 s)

**Hook**
Two thousand people stepped onto a brand-new bridge, and it started to dance.

**Setting**
London, June 2000. The Millennium Bridge, a sleek steel ribbon across the Thames, opens to huge crowds. The engineers are proud. Nothing like it has ever been built.

**Action**
Step one: a tiny sway. Step two: people notice. Step three: they spread their feet to keep their balance. Now every step pushes the bridge sideways, in time with everyone else.

**Snap**
The bridge wobbles harder. So people step in sync even more. So it wobbles harder still.

**Deadpan double line**
Luckily, it never fell. Unluckily, everyone was clinging to the handrails like they were on a boat.

**Explanation**
Think of a swing. A small push every time it comes back, and soon it's flying. Here, the crowd was the pusher, and nobody planned it.

**Consequence**
Two days later, they closed it. Engineers added dampers to soak up the motion, and it reopened in 2002.

**Callback twist**
Today it's rock solid. Londoners still call it the Wobbly Bridge.
