# Shot plan: What If You Could See Everyone's Body Count?

Format: 1920x1080 landscape, 30 fps, 4:58 (about 8,930 frames).
Timings come from `voice/timeline.json` (sentence times) and `voice/words.json` (word times for captions). Narration is locked (Fenrir, v3 natural pacing).
Style: the WHAT IF 2D stick-figure look with the **visual upgrade v2** (see `channel-kit/VISUAL_UPGRADE_V2.md`): 3-layer parallax depth, soft ground shadows, blinking and reacting characters, per-section palettes, eased counters, whip/iris transitions, a few props per scene. **Do not overdo it:** at most 3 animated elements at once, at most 4 colours per scene, no particle storms, quiet backgrounds during key numbers.

## Guardrails for this topic (important)
- **Strictly non-explicit.** No nudity, no suggestive poses, no bedroom scenes, no innuendo in the visuals. Intimacy is never depicted; only numbers, faces and reactions. Stick figures stay fully dressed and in everyday settings.
- **The number is a neutral floating counter** above each head (a plain rounded tag with a digit). It is never styled as a sexual symbol.
- **No shaming visuals.** No one is mocked or punished for their number. Reactions are surprise, relief, curiosity, awkwardness. Varied, ordinary stick figures (all genders, ages, body types, skin tones), no stereotypes.
- **No real people, no real brand logos.** The "Instagram like count" scene uses a generic social-feed mock-up with a heart icon and a hidden count; no Instagram logo or brand colours.
- **"What a number can't say" (3:44-4:02):** keep this scene very gentle and abstract: a single quiet figure, a soft light, a thought bubble that closes, then opens as a speech bubble ("tell the story yourself"). No depiction of any harm, no people in distress, no dramatic music.
- **Clare's Law (4:02-4:19):** show a plain document/shield icon and a safety-themed "right to ask" card; no police or violence imagery beyond a neutral badge-free shield; no real insignia.
- **Culture neutral:** do not tie any reaction to a specific culture or religion. "Different families and cultures" is shown as varied dinner-table reactions with generic figures.
- **Honest labels:** the 2003 lie-detector study appears as a small card "small study, 2003"; the median appears as "typical (median): about 3-4".

## Recurring visual devices
- **The number tag:** a rounded tag floating and bobbing above every head. It animates in (squash-and-stretch pop) on the hook, stays on every figure from then on, and is the visual spine of the video.
- **The crowd:** a few background figures with their own tags, parallax at lower contrast so the foreground reads first.
- **Counters:** eased roll-ups for numbers (3-4 median, top 5%, 50 cap, 2003, 2019, 2014).
- **The mirror moment:** a figure looking at their own tag in a mirror-like frame (used once, in the rules).

## Dead air rules (from the silence audit)
Whenever the narration pauses 0.7 s or longer, something on screen keeps moving (tags bobbing, a slow push-in, a counter ticking, a parallax drift), but quiet during the two `[beat]` pauses (after "Nobody can hide it." and "Imagine a number floating above your head."), which may hold a still frame for about 1.2 s with a slow push-in.

## Build order (Claude Code on the Mac)
1. Build sections 1-3 (0:00-0:53) as the **look test**: render 6 half-size stills and wait for approval (check the visual-upgrade limits: depth, shadows, no more than 3 animated elements).
2. Build the remaining sections back to back, then show 10-12 stills across them.
3. Sound mix, then a half-resolution draft render, then ask before the full render.
4. Full render (keep the final audio peak below -1 dBFS after AAC), frame-by-frame quality check, SRT subtitle file.
